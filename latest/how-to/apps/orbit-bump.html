Example: Orbit Bump#

The orbit bump app is the example app OSPREY ships; the demo preset leaves it off. It solves a local closed-orbit bump — move the beam at one or more BPMs by a chosen offset and close the bump with the surrounding correctors — on the session’s control target, shows the plan, and applies it behind an approval. The operator works in the Orbit bump panel; the agent uses a tool for each panel action, on the same backend. Both see one plan per login, control target and model: the plan the agent computes is the plan the panel shows and the plan the approval card binds.

This page reads the app three ways: as the operator, as the agent, and as its source — the worked example for Build Your Own App.

To turn it on, initialise with osprey init --preset demo --set 'apps.orbit-bump={}': init copies the app into apps/orbit-bump/ and lists it in profile.yml as orbit-bump: {} under apps:. The model dropdown offers the ring models that solve a closed orbit — a served pyat model not set to single_pass — so a facility without one has nothing to pick. The app sets a bump and leaves it; to step a bump through amplitudes and measure the orbit shift at each, use Bluesky’s orbit_bump_sweep plan (Run Your First Plan).

From the panel#

1. Pick the plane and model. Horizontal or Vertical; the model dropdown lists the ring models with a closed-orbit model. The chip beside it shows the state of the model’s orbit response matrix (ORM).

2. Pick the BPMs. Drag across the dots on the strip to add every BPM in the range; click a dot to toggle it, ⇧-click to extend from the last one picked. Or type in the picker: 22, 21-23 (a range by ring position), 2* (a prefix). ⇧-drag, the wheel and a double-click zoom; ⌥-drag pans. Right-click the strip for the same verbs; the ☰ menu lists the gestures.

3. Set the offsets. Each target is an offset from golden, in the facility’s BPM unit — not an absolute position and not a change from the current reading. Set them with the amplitude and shape (flat, peak, cosine) over the bump BPMs, by dragging a ◆ handle or the bump’s grip (moves every target), with ↑/↓ on a focused handle or row (⇧ ×10), or by typing in the table. The nudge step (1, 10, 100 µm) is in the ☰ menu.

Other BPMs on the Bump card sets what every BPM not in the bump aims at, inside the corrector span and outside it: free (unconstrained), hold (default; its reading at Compute) or zero (golden). The strip rings each held BPM at its aim.

4. Choose the corrector rule.

Rule

Correctors

auto

one upstream of the first BPM, every corrector between the BPMs, two downstream of the last

tight

the MML signed-index rule [-1 1], relative to the bump BPMs

manual

the correctors you click on the strip, or drag across on its corrector row: a drag adds the range, or removes it when every one is selected; under another rule it switches to manual from the plan’s correctors

The ⋯ menu after the toggle adds two wider MML lists, [-2 -1 1 2] and [-3 -1 1 3], and Use all correctors of the plane, which overrides the rule (an all correctors chip shows it). On a large ring the approval card then shows a summary line, the largest-|Δ| rows and every row without a tolerance in full, and names all other correctors; the panel’s confirm card lists every row.

5. Compute. Compute writes nothing. It replaces your current plan with a new one and draws the predicted orbit and each corrector’s step under the strip. Editing the bump afterwards marks it edited · not computed and holds Apply until the next Compute. A bump BPM that cannot be read, or reads a non-finite value, blocks compute and is named.

After a Compute the Correctors card head shows the solve’s singular values on a log scale, the kept ones filled, and a cutoff with σ k/n. Drag the cutoff, or press ←/→ on it, to keep fewer or more; each change computes again. Fewer gives smaller, smoother deltas and a looser fit. By default the solve keeps the values above 10⁻⁴ of the largest. Under hit targets the target step’s values come first and the closure step’s after, so a lower count drops closure before targets.

6. Read the card. The Correctors card has one row per corrector: now, delta, new and the limit verdict; a row’s note is its tooltip, and the closure line is the tooltip of the card’s title. The confirmation shows all of it, with the channel each row writes.

  • Verdict: the declared limit range, no declared limit, limits checking off, or a refusal with the overshoot (over by …).

  • Note: no tolerance: not staleness-checked marks a setpoint without a declared tolerance; not settled shows a readback that has not reached its setpoint.

  • Closure line: singular values kept (σ kept, on the confirmation), max predicted leakage at the held BPMs, dropped other BPMs, BPMs skipped because the ORM could not solve them, and correctors left at a limit (at limit).

Unreachable bumps. The solve keeps every corrector inside its declared limits (range and step). When the correctors cannot reach the targets — a limit, or too few correctors to hold the other BPMs — the plan still computes: a warning chip gives the largest miss, missed BPMs show their predicted value in red and a red ring on their handle, and the confirmation names the miss. Priority in the Correctors ⋯ menu trades the two: close bump (default) holds the other BPMs first; hit targets meets the targets where the correctors can and lets the other BPMs leak. A chip names hit targets.

7. Apply. Apply… is enabled only on a ready, unedited plan while the session’s machine allows writes. It opens a confirmation with the setpoints and the closure line; Confirm writes the rows once, in plan order.

8. Iterate. After a full apply the plan is applied, the orbit is re-read, and the Correctors title’s tooltip shows residual after at the bump BPMs. To refine, compute again on the re-read orbit and apply the new plan. Every write is on a confirmed or approved plan.

Recompute on the ORM chip rebuilds the orbit response matrix from the model; nothing is measured. A new ORM discards every login’s plans on that model not yet applied (ready and not_applicable); compute again.

With writes off for the session’s machine the panel names the reason (Writes are off) and refuses apply; compute, discard and recompute still work, so a bump can be planned and inspected. On a login that does not arm the app the panel shows state and the approval only.

From the agent#

Every panel element has a tool or an argument (Skills and agent parity):

Tool

Does

bump_state(model, plane, bpms)

Reads what the panel shows: per BPM the orbit, golden, target, prediction and miss; the models and their ORM status and age; the corrector ticks; the posture; the current plan; the operator’s draft; and what holds Apply, in the panel’s words, with the approval card’s lines. bpms narrows it to a stretch of the ring.

plan_bump(model, plane, targets, rule, correctors, priority, others, all_correctors, sigma_keep, bpms, amplitude, shape)

Compute. Writes nothing; replaces the current plan and returns it. targets maps BPM to offset from golden, or bpms, amplitude and shape spread it as the panel does; rule is auto, mml:[…], or manual with correctors. priority, others, all_correctors and sigma_keep (the singular-value cutoff) are the panel’s choices; missed and target_miss_max report what the plan does not reach.

discard_bump()

Clear: drops the current plan.

recompute_orm(model)

recompute on the ORM chip.

apply_bump(plan_id)

Writes the plan. Runs only behind an approval bound to that plan id and its setpoints. A plan id that is not current is refused, naming the current plan.

Ask the agent to move BPM 7 up by 0.2 mm in the vertical plane. It calls plan_bump, reports the plan — the correctors, each setpoint with its limit verdict, the predicted residual — and then asks to apply. At asked the approval card appears with the plan id and the corrector rows; the operator’s approval is the confirmation. At allowed the apply runs bound to the same plan, with no card. If apply_bump gets no answer in time, it reports the plan status and the row outcomes the backend holds rather than retrying.

The panel publishes the operator’s selection as a draft a moment after each edit, and bump_state returns it, so the agent sees a bump before it is computed. The draft is kept in memory per login, is not published on a login that does not arm the app, and only the panel posts it.

The app’s skill tells the agent all of this — which tool for which request, that offsets are from golden, to show the plan before asking to apply, what each status and refusal means — so an operator can ask in the facility’s words and the agent uses the tools as the panel would.

How it is built#

The app is a demo deployment’s apps/orbit-bump/: the three parts of Build Your Own OSPREY Apps, with the manifest:

# A local closed-orbit bump: a panel and a tool per panel action over one backend; the write is the plan's apply.
name: orbit-bump
summary: Solves a local orbit bump over a ring's BPMs and correctors and applies it on approval.
containers:
  orbit-bump:
    runtime: osprey
    command: [python, -m, orbit_bump.server]
    health: /health
    drives_target: true
    control_identity: true
    state: var/orbit-bump
panel:
  label: Orbit bump
  container: orbit-bump
  launch_token: true
  # An apply writes every corrector, waits for the readbacks to settle and re-reads the orbit.
  read_timeout_s: 90
  agent_routes:
    - [GET, /api/state]
    - [GET, /api/approval]
    - [POST, /api/recompute]
    - [POST, /api/discard]
    - [POST, /api/compute]
    - [POST, /api/apply]
  roster_grant: {single_user_alias: true}
tools:
  module: orbit_bump.tools
  deny_in_jobs: true
  # Only apply writes a channel; discard drops a stored plan, recompute rebuilds a model matrix.
  read: [bump_state, plan_bump, discard_bump, recompute_orm]
  approval:
    - tool: apply_bump
      prompt: Apply bump plan {{ state.plan_id }} (the call names plan {{ input.plan_id }}).
      state_path: /api/approval
      display_lines_key: display_lines
      bind:
        state_fields:
          - {field: approved_plan_id, key: plan_id}
          - {field: approved_setpoints, key: setpoint_digest}
      require:
        all_of: [approved_target, approved_plan_id, approved_setpoints]
  errors:
    unreachable: The orbit bump panel's backend is not answering; check its container with osprey health.
    refused: The bump was not applied. Compute it again and ask for a new approval.
skills: [orbit-bump]

The backend (orbit_bump/server.py) keeps the plans in a store under the container’s state directory, one current plan per login, target and model, so a plan survives a page reload and a restart. It knows no facility of its own: the ring models, their BPMs and correctors and each model’s response matrix come from the SDK (model_members, model_response_matrix), the orbit and golden orbit are read through the connector, and the limit verdict and tolerance of each setpoint come from the SDK’s pre-write verdict (prewrite_verdict), the same judgement the connector makes at the write. Six routes are the agent’s: GET /api/state (models, orbit, posture, the current plan, the draft), GET /api/approval (what the card binds), and POST /api/recompute, /api/discard, /api/compute and /api/apply. The panel’s POST /api/draft is not an agent route.

The tool server (orbit_bump/tools.py) has one tool per panel action. bump_state reads /api/state and /api/approval; plan_bump, discard_bump and recompute_orm post to their routes and are read tools, because none writes a channel. apply_bump runs check_approved_call, takes the plan id and setpoint digest the approval bound, refuses a plan_id that differs from the approved one, and posts both to /api/apply with a timeout long enough for the writes to settle and the orbit to be re-read — which is why the manifest’s read_timeout_s is 90.

The approval entry is the heart of the example. state_path is a route made for the card, /api/approval: it answers only while the current plan is ready and fits a card, with the plan id, a digest of the setpoints and display_lines, one per corrector. The card shows those lines, so the operator approves every setpoint, not a summary. bind.state_fields copies the plan id and the digest into the call, and the backend applies only when both still match the plan it holds. A plan recomputed after approval, or approved on another machine, is refused without a write.

The panel (panel/) is the strip, the model and ORM chip, the plan card and the confirm dialog, in plain JavaScript on the terminal’s design tokens. It reads the backend’s posture and shows Writes are off rather than hiding the controls. Its tests under tests/ run with osprey app check.

The skill (skills/orbit-bump/SKILL.md) is listed under skills: and installed into every login that shows the app.

deny_in_jobs: true keeps every tool out of dispatch jobs: a bump is an operator’s decision. control_identity: true makes the container’s writes reach the control system under the person’s name rather than the shared osprey account.

Plan states#

Status

Meaning

ready

Can be applied.

applying

Being written; cannot be discarded until it ends.

not_applicable

A new setpoint violates a declared limit, or the missed targets and closure do not fit the approval card. The card states the reason. Change targets or rule and compute again.

superseded

Replaced by a newer plan.

discarded

Dropped, for instance by a new ORM.

applied

Every row confirmed; the orbit was re-read.

partially_applied

A row did not confirm and the sequence stopped. Cannot be re-applied; compute a new plan from the current state.

Row outcomes#

After an apply each row carries an outcome with its readback. Any outcome other than confirmed stops the sequence.

Outcome

Meaning

confirmed

The connector confirmed the write.

mismatch

Written; the readback disagrees.

refused

The write was refused.

failed

The write failed.

unconfirmed

Hardware state unknown.

not attempted

An earlier row stopped the sequence.

Refusals#

Nothing is written when an apply is refused.

  • Stale. A corrector’s readback moved since compute by more than the tolerance its setpoint declares. Compute again and approve the new plan. Rows marked no tolerance: not staleness-checked are not checked. Orbit motion since compute is not staleness.

  • Writes off. With writes off for the session’s machine, apply and apply_bump are refused and the panel names the reason; compute and plan_bump still plan. See Writes and approvals.

  • Unarmed login. Reads state and approvals only; compute, discard and recompute are refused.

  • Owner, target or model differ from the plan’s: refused. Compute on the current target and model.

  • Approval missing or for another plan: refused. Approve the current plan.
